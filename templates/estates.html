Why the land carries value

A ridge this close to the coast is not reproducible.

Coastal St. Ann is built out at the shoreline and climbing inland fast. Land above Ocho Rios with an ocean aspect, live spring water and mature hardwood canopy is a finite position — the parcels that exist are the parcels that will ever exist.

Inside the estate the whole subdivision has already been surveyed, laid out and engineered. Access roads, lot boundaries and building envelopes are drawn. A buyer is not paying for raw bush and a promise; the expensive, slow, technical work is behind the land, not ahead of it.

Covenants do the rest. Setbacks, material palette and density limits keep the canopy intact, which protects both the view and the resale argument for every parcel on the ridge.

Pre-development · Phase one

The whole ridge, not a single pad.

One hundred acres read as a single plan: a switchback spine road, terraced building envelopes held in disciplined rows, canopy kept as a green matrix between every one of them, an arrival court and pavilion at the head of the drive, trails and two retention ponds in the gullies. Nothing is improvised lot by lot.

The community, not just the parcel

A handful of villas, one walkway, and nothing else on the ridge.

Low density by design. Homes step down the slope on an elevated hardwood walk, sharing a fire pavilion and the view — the shape of a small eco-retreat rather than a subdivision.

MaterialsLocal limestone, stained hardwood, brass, no ornament. The palette is fixed by covenant so the ridge reads as one place in thirty years, not thirty houses.
