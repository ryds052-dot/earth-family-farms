Why here

Inland from Ocho Rios, above the noise of the coast.

Collier sits in the hills of Bonham Spring, St. Ann — minutes from Ocho Rios and Tower Isle, yet held in cool highland air. A natural gully runs the length of the ground, bordered by main-road frontage on the west and an internal 12.19m spine road climbing the eastern ridge.

The slow groundwork is already done: surveyed at 1:1500, laid out around the Earth Family compound, J.P.S. power corridor and gully, and submitted for statutory review. You are not looking at raw bush.

The plan

The whole ridge, not a single pad.

Sixty-one acres held in eco-tourism and canopy, thirty-seven acres planned for low-density ridge places, and three acres in the initial pre-development release.

Architecture & Livity

Stone below, hardwood above, glass to the drop.

The covenant fixes the language, not your floor plan: local limestone, stained hardwood, brass, no ornament. Setbacks and height keep every house low enough for the Bonham Spring canopy to stay the horizon.

The drawings and a contractor shortlist come with the plan. You are not handed an empty slope and a set of rules.

The village

Earth Family compound and a fire pavilion. Nothing else.

Homes step down an elevated hardwood walk toward the gully and the shared room at the head of the ridge.
